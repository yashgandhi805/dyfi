Lumpsum vs SIP — Which is Better?

✅ Lumpsum Advantages

  • • Higher potential returns in a bull market
  • • Entire amount works from Day 1
  • • Better when you have a large idle corpus
  • • Suitable when markets are undervalued

⚠️ Lumpsum Risks

  • • Risk of investing at market peak (timing risk)
  • • Requires large capital upfront
  • • Volatility can be more impactful
  • • Consider STP for gradual equity deployment

For most salaried investors, SIP is recommended. For those with a lumpsum (bonus, inheritance), consider STP or direct lumpsum after market analysis. Always consult a SEBI-registered investment advisor.

What is Lumpsum?

A lumpsum investment is a one-time, single large investment into a mutual fund scheme, as opposed to periodic investments through SIP. The entire amount is invested at once.

How is it Calculated?

Using compound interest formula: A = P × (1 + r)ⁿ, where P = principal, r = annual rate, n = years. This assumes compounding annually at a constant rate.

LTCG Tax on Equity Funds

Gains from equity funds held over 1 year are taxed at 12.5% (LTCG) above ₹1.25 lakh per year. Debt fund gains are taxed as per income slab. Tax laws may change; consult a tax advisor.

Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, please read all scheme related documents carefully.
